CROWN Acts and labour-market outcomes

Do protections against hair discrimination change Black women's employment, earnings, or occupational opportunities?

Area
Selected research
Methods & tools
Econometrics · Python · ACS
View code on GitHub ↗

The question

A narrowly targeted anti-discrimination policy may change individual hiring interactions without producing a large movement in broad labour-market measures. This paper studies that distinction using US state CROWN laws and American Community Survey data.

Data and design

The paper uses IPUMS ACS data for 2015–2024, constructing weighted state-by-year-by-race/sex cells for non-Hispanic Black and White adults aged 25–64. A triple-difference specification combines state-year, state-group and year-group fixed effects, with standard errors clustered by state.

Employment, participation, self-employment, wage income and occupational measures address different possible margins. The implementation provides weighted cell preparation and estimation of the paper's core specification.

Reported findings

The paper reports small employment, participation and self-employment estimates. Wage and occupational results require a more qualified reading: broad averages do not isolate the specific hiring mechanism, and composition can affect observed earnings.

These findings concern the measured outcomes and sample window. They do not establish that hair discrimination disappeared or that the laws had no effect.

My contribution

Independent research connecting a policy question, microdata construction and an econometric design. The repository documents the input schema, estimator and reported findings.
