The research idea

Could adaptive statistical methods and thoughtful testing workflows make visual-field assessment more efficient and comfortable? I am exploring this through sequential threshold estimation and a review of testing algorithms and patient experience.

The code

An offline simulator represents synthetic observers with probabilistic responses, then compares a staircase procedure with a discrete Bayesian estimator. It provides a way to explore presentation budgets, response errors and stopping rules.

This is a simulation framework, not a validated clinical test or a replacement for a Humphrey Field Analyzer. No patient outcomes or clinical performance are claimed.

Starting literature

  • Heijl et al. (2019): SITA Faster and established efficiency benchmarks
  • Muthusamy et al. (2020): adaptive spatial sampling with ARREST
  • qVFM (2021): Bayesian active learning in simulated observers
  • Lu et al. (2024): patient experience and testing barriers
  • Open Perimetry Initiative: research software and interfaces

Further questions

Patient instructions, pacing and accessible hardware deserve investigation alongside algorithms. Their effects would require separate studies; a simulation cannot demonstrate comfort or clinical reliability.
