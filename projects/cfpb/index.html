Complaint language and measured redress

Template reuse, text classification, and what consumer complaint records can tell us about reported relief.

Area
Selected research
Methods & tools
NLP · Python · Measurement
View code on GitHub ↗

The question

Complaint narratives are both descriptions of consumer problems and products of how people write or obtain assistance. I am interested in how repeated wording and complaint composition affect what researchers infer from the CFPB database.

Implementation

The exploratory work uses TF-IDF, clustering, sentence embeddings and similarity measures. Later scripts add reusable text features, duplicate/template summaries and outcome models. A connected research proposal develops human-reviewed measurement and sensitivity to selection into narrative publication.

What the work shows

The saved exploratory work identifies substantial text reuse and differences between filtered complaint groups. These patterns motivate further measurement work; they do not establish AI authorship, fraudulent complaints, or a causal benefit of a writing style.

Monetary relief, non-monetary relief and durable resolution are distinct outcomes. Company-reported closure categories cannot establish that a consumer's underlying problem was fixed.

Background and acknowledgement

This began as formally assessed DS202W coursework at LSE and developed into an independent project. I am grateful to Dr Ghita Berrada for valuable feedback and help iterating on the work.
